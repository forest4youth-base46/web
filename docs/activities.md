# Pocketbook of Activities — Full Content

Every activity in the pocketbook (17 total), grouped by the five therapeutic-arc themes. Use `activities.csv` for bulk editing in a spreadsheet.

Adaptations for specific presentations appear at the end.

## Group 1 · Getting There

### Introduce Yourself to the Forest  *(id: `introduce`, 10–20 min, avg 15)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A name spelled out in sticks, leaves and stones
- **Purpose:** Build a personal connection with the forest. Find a place of your own.
- **Materials:** None — the forest provides.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Walk around and find a spot that speaks to you. Spell out your name there using natural materials — as big, small, simple or ornate as you feel today. A nickname or initial is fine."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Signal the group back to a meeting point. Invite anyone who wants to share.
- **Tags:** `First session`, `Individual`, `Anxiety`

### The Soundscape Map  *(id: `soundscape`, 10–15 min, avg 12)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Sound sources marked radiating from the listener's position
- **Purpose:** Engage the senses on arrival.
- **Materials:** Paper, pencil, clipboard per person.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Find your own spot. Sit still for five minutes. Mark where you are, then mark where sounds come from and how they feel. Share anything you want when we come back."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Bring the group together. Invite sharing. Ask: did you hear anything unexpected?
- **Tags:** `First session`, `Groups`, `Anxiety`

### Naming the Forest  *(id: `naming`, 5–10 min, avg 7)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Forest elements identified and named along the path
- **Purpose:** Names reduce anxiety. Familiarity builds confidence.
- **Materials:** Nothing, or a laminated sheet of local species.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "As we walk I'll point a few things out. You don't need to remember any of it. Everything here has a name."
- **Close (facilitator note):** No formal close. Dissolves into the walk.
- **Tags:** `First session`, `Anxiety`

### The Hammock  *(id: `hammock`, 30–60 min, avg 45)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A person resting in a hammock between two trees
- **Purpose:** Settle into the woodland.
- **Materials:** One or more hammocks hung between trees.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "There's a hammock over there. Go and lie in it if you want. You don't have to do or say anything. Just notice what it's like. We'll check in every half hour."
- **Close (facilitator note):** No structured close. Let young people leave when ready. Have the next activity quietly ready.
- **Tags:** `Individual`, `Anxiety`, `Trauma-informed`

## Group 2 · Waking Up Your Senses

### Barefoot Trail  *(id: `barefoot`, 10–15 min, avg 12)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Five distinct textures along a barefoot path
- **Purpose:** Engages touch and proprioception. Grounds ruminative or anxious thinking.
- **Materials:** A short varied path of grass, soil, bark, moss, stone.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "If you're comfortable, take off your shoes. We're going to walk this bit very slowly. Just notice what you feel through your feet."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Pause the group. Ask: what was the most interesting texture? What surprised you?
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Anxiety`, `Somatic work`

### Colour Palette Walk  *(id: `palette`, 15–20 min, avg 17)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A colour palette card with forest elements matched to each swatch
- **Purpose:** Focused looking without needing words.
- **Materials:** Laminated colour palette or paint chips per person.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "As we walk, try to find something in the forest that matches each colour. You don't need to pick anything up, just find it."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Gather the group. Who found a surprising match? Seasonal variation: make a palette from fallen leaves.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Children`, `Creative work`

### Five Senses Inventory  *(id: `senses`, 5–10 min, avg 7)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** 5-4-3-2-1 grounding sequence
- **Purpose:** Structured grounding (5-4-3-2-1). Produces quiet, focused attention.
- **Materials:** Nothing, or a small prompt card.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Stand still. Find five things you can see. Four you can hear. Three you can feel. Two you can smell. One you can taste or imagine tasting. Take your time."
- **Close (facilitator note):** No formal close. Can be used as a reset at any point.
- **Tags:** `Anxiety`, `Trauma-informed`, `Somatic work`

### Build a Tiny World  *(id: `tinyworld`, 45–60 min, avg 52)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Mud, sticks and leaves built into an imagined world
- **Purpose:** Hands-on play, creativity and group immersion. Encourages touch, imagination and collaborative making.
- **Materials:** Natural materials on site: mud, soil, leaves, sticks, stones, bark, moss, water.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "We're going to create an imaginary world using only what we can find. The theme is Build A Tiny World. Work in small groups for about an hour. There's no right or wrong way."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Signal the end a few minutes early. Invite groups to walk through each other's worlds. Return materials to the ground. Invite reflections.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Children`, `Creative work`

## Group 3 · Discovering Yourself

### Building the Forest Sofa  *(id: `sofa`, 30–45 min, avg 37)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A collaborative seating circle built from logs and branches
- **Purpose:** Create a shared space owned by the group. A gathering point for the rest of the programme.
- **Materials:** Fallen branches and logs. No tools needed.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "We're going to make somewhere to sit that fits all of us. See what you can find — branches, logs, anything on the ground — and bring it back here."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Sit in it together. Mark the moment. Return to it in future sessions.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Connection building`, `Relational work`

### Fire Lighting  *(id: `fire`, 45–60 min, avg 52)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Tinder · kindling · structure · flame
- **Purpose:** Shared goal, skill transfer, visible outcome.
- **Materials:** Site permission, safety clearance, competent practitioner. Fire steel or matches, tinder, kindling, fuel wood, fire circle, water nearby.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "We're going to light a fire. I'll show you how to set it up, then anyone who wants to can try. It usually takes a few goes — it can be tricky!"
- **Close (facilitator note):** Formal close required. Extinguish fully together. Every participant should see this. Leave the site as found.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Adolescents`, `Connection building`

### Bivouac Building  *(id: `bivouac`, 45–60 min, avg 52)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Ridge pole · two anchor points · tensioned tarp
- **Purpose:** Cooperation, problem-solving, shared accomplishment.
- **Materials:** Tarpaulins and rope, or on-site materials.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "We're going to build a shelter big enough for at least four of us. There's no one right way. See what you've got and what you can find. I'll help if you get stuck."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Test it together. Sit inside briefly. Mark the achievement.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Adolescents`, `Relational work`

## Group 4 · Connecting with Others

### The Sit Spot  *(id: `sitspot`, 15–20 min, avg 17)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Each person finds a personal place, returned to each session
- **Purpose:** Each young person finds a place in the forest that is theirs, returned to across sessions.
- **Materials:** 15–20 minutes unstructured. Site with enough variety for individual spots within earshot.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Find somewhere in the forest that speaks to you. Somewhere you'd want to sit. It can be anywhere. You don't need to explain why. We'll come back together in fifteen minutes."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Invite anyone to show or describe their spot. Make returning to it a regular ritual.
- **Tags:** `Individual`, `Adolescents`, `Identity work`

### Distributed Responsibility  *(id: `roles`, 0–0 min, avg 0)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Small functional roles distributed across the group
- **Purpose:** Young people experience being depended on, not just cared for.
- **Materials:** Specific practical roles before departure: who carries cups, tea, checks the path, holds the map.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "I need some help today. I can't do this alone. I'm going to ask each of you to be responsible for something."
- **Close (facilitator note):** At the end, name what each person did specifically. This is acknowledgement of a real contribution.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Adolescents`, `Connection building`

### The Individual Project  *(id: `project`, 0–0 min, avg 0)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A personal project evolving across sessions
- **Purpose:** One young person takes on a personal project across multiple sessions.
- **Materials:** Session time, on-site materials.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "This part of the forest isn't being used for anything. I wondered if you might want to do something with it over the next few weeks? Build something, plant something, create something?"
- **Close (facilitator note):** At programme end, the group visits together. The young person leads and describes what they made and why.
- **Tags:** `Individual`, `Adolescents`, `Identity work`

## Group 5 · Coming Back to Yourself

### The Forest Object  *(id: `object`, 5–10 min, avg 7)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** Each person selects a small object to carry forward — one word for what it holds
- **Purpose:** A small natural object to carry between sessions — a portable resource.
- **Materials:** Permission to take small objects. A bag or envelope per person.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Before we go, find something small you want to take with you. Something from today. You don't need to explain why."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Invite anyone to show what they chose and say one word about it. One word only — not a reflection exercise.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Individual`, `Identity work`, `Life transitions`

### The Return Check-in  *(id: `checkin`, 2–5 min, avg 3)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A private self-assessment quietly collected before the walk back
- **Purpose:** Brief self-assessment before leaving. Builds self-reflection as a habit.
- **Materials:** A simple rating card: much better / a bit better / about the same / a bit worse.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Fill this in before we get on the bus. You don't need to share it. Just take a moment to notice."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Collect cards quietly. Begin the walk back. Allow quiet on the return — don't process the session immediately.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Session check-in`, `Anxiety`

### Campfire Close  *(id: `campfire`, 10–15 min, avg 12)*

- **Caption (under illustration):** A circle around the fire, shared stillness before departure
- **Purpose:** A natural closing ritual. Shared stillness before departure.
- **Materials:** A fire from the session, or tea on a small stove if no fire.
- **Intro (facilitator script):** "Before we put the fire out, let's sit with it for a few minutes. You don't have to say anything. Just notice where you are. Then we'll head back."
- **Close (facilitator note):** Extinguish the fire together. Walk back however the group chooses.
- **Tags:** `Groups`, `Session check-in`

## Adaptations for specific presentations

### High anxiety

Prioritise activities with a clear task and visible outcome over open-ended activities in early sessions. The colour palette walk, distributed responsibility, and the sit spot all provide structure without requiring performance. Avoid activities requiring physical proximity to others or closed/dark spaces until trust in the setting is established. Graduate sensory intensity across the programme arc.

### Autistic young people

Predictability of structure is paramount. Describe the session arc in advance, ideally using a visual schedule. Assess sensory sensitivities before any sensory activity: the barefoot trail and activities involving unfamiliar textures should be offered as genuine options, not defaults. Many autistic young people show strong intrinsic interest in natural systems; the naming activity and colour palette walk often produce strong engagement. Allow extended time where genuine absorption occurs.

### Very low mood

The primary aim in early sessions is physical presence in the natural environment, not activity completion. The hammock, the fire, and the sit spot are the most appropriate starting points. Frame success as arriving and staying rather than participating, to reduce the risk of a first session that confirms the young person's belief that they cannot engage.

### High hyperactivity

Use physical and task-based activities early in the session to allow discharge of arousal before quieter activities are introduced. Bivouac building, fire preparation, and distributed responsibility all provide legitimate physical engagement. Transition to quieter activities gradually: moderate activity, then a short focused activity, then genuinely unstructured time.

### First session resistance

Use the lightest possible structure: no requirements, an explicit offer to sit out, and a guide who does not appear to be managing the resistant young person. Giving a resistant young person a specific and optional responsibility — one requiring minimal engagement but placing them adjacent to the group activity — often provides a route in that confrontational approaches do not.
